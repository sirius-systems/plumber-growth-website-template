## 1. Purpose

This document defines which plumbing companies are most likely to:

- Need the Plumber Growth System
- Understand its value
- Complete onboarding successfully
- Use the system consistently
- Generate visible operational activity
- Remain subscribed
- Fit the standardized $297-per-month delivery model

The goal is not to sell the product to every plumbing business. The goal is to identify companies where the product can address a clear and existing operational problem.

---

## 2. Ideal Customer Summary

The ideal customer is an established local plumbing company with legitimate customers, an active Google Business Profile, existing service demand, and an outdated or disconnected website and lead-response process.

The company is already capable of completing plumbing work. Its main weakness is capturing, responding to, organizing, and following up with customer opportunities.

### One-sentence profile

> An established local plumbing company with existing demand and customer credibility that loses opportunities because its website, missed-call handling, lead follow-up, pipeline, and review-request processes are weak or disconnected.

---

## 3. Core Firmographic Profile

### Business type

Prioritize:

- Independent plumbing companies
- Residential plumbing companies
- Residential and light-commercial plumbers
- Emergency plumbing providers
- Drain and sewer companies with broader plumbing services
- Water-heater specialists offering general plumbing
- Family-owned plumbing businesses
- Owner-operated plumbing companies with a small team

### Geographic market

Initial market:

- United States
- Defined local or regional service area
- One primary location
- One Google Business Profile
- One primary operating brand

Multi-location plumbing companies may require a higher plan because they create additional website, GBP, routing, reporting, and workflow complexity.

### Recommended business age

Preferred:

- At least two years in operation

Acceptable:

- At least one year when the business has existing customers, legitimate reviews, and demonstrated demand

Avoid:

- Businesses launching without customers, reviews, history, or operational capacity

### Recommended company size

Preferred initial range:

- 2–20 employees
- 1–10 service vehicles
- Owner plus technicians or office support
- One primary decision-maker

Smaller owner-operators may qualify when they have strong demand but poor administrative systems.

Larger companies may require more advanced permissions, routing, reporting, dispatch integration, and support.

## 4. Revenue and Job-Economics Profile

The product does not require access to exact financial information during initial prospecting, but the customer should have meaningful job economics.

Preferred characteristics:

- A typical completed job can produce enough gross profit to justify a $297 monthly system
- The company completes recurring monthly jobs
- The company can fulfill additional service requests
- The owner understands the cost of missed calls and forgotten estimates
- The company is not relying on one or two jobs per year

Potential high-value services include:

- Emergency plumbing
- Water-heater replacement
- Sewer-line repair
- Repiping
- Leak detection and repair
- Drain and sewer services
- Commercial plumbing
- Fixture installations
- Remodel-related plumbing

Do not use job-value estimates as guarantees.

## 5. Digital-Maturity Profile

### Preferred digital foundation

The ideal customer has:

- An active Google Business Profile
- Legitimate customer reviews
- A business telephone number
- A domain or willingness to use one
- An existing website, even if outdated
- A business email address or willingness to establish one
- A primary employee responsible for new inquiries
- Basic comfort using a mobile application

### Common website weaknesses

Strong prospects may have:

- No website
- An outdated website
- Slow mobile performance
- Broken forms
- Weak calls to action
- No dedicated request-service process
- No emergency-request process
- Forms that only send email
- No immediate acknowledgment
- Inconsistent business information
- Missing service pages
- Weak service-area content
- No clear review integration
- A website provider that responds slowly
- A website the plumber cannot update

### Common operational weaknesses

Strong prospects may also have:

- Missed calls without follow-up
- Website leads buried in email
- No centralized conversation history
- Estimates tracked in memory or spreadsheets
- No consistent follow-up process
- No appointment reminders
- No review-request workflow
- No clear lead attribution
- Multiple employees responding inconsistently
- No visibility into unclosed opportunities

## 6. Google Business Profile Profile

### Strong-fit indicators

The best prospects may have:

- An established profile
- Correct primary category
- Legitimate recent reviews
- Consistent business information
- Active phone calls from Google
- Strong customer satisfaction
- Weak website support behind the profile

### Example high-priority prospect

> A plumber with 75 legitimate Google reviews, a profile active for several years, a basic or outdated website, and no immediate lead-response system.

### Acceptable development prospect

> A plumber with 10–30 legitimate reviews, multiple years of customer history, an unoptimized website, and a customer database that can support honest review requests.

### Weak-fit indicators

- No profile
- No reviews
- No customer history
- Suspended or misleading listing
- Fake location
- Keyword-stuffed business name
- Unwillingness to correct policy violations
- Request to manufacture reviews

## 7. Primary Buyer Roles

### 7.1 Owner-operator

#### Responsibilities

- Plumbing work
- Estimates
- Customer calls
- Scheduling
- Business decisions

#### Pain points

- Cannot answer while working
- Misses calls
- Forgets follow-up
- Has no time to learn software
- Distrusts marketing agencies
- Wants a simple, practical system

#### Effective message

> We install and manage the website and follow-up system so you can focus on plumbing work without every new inquiry getting lost in your phone or email.

### 7.2 Growth-focused owner

#### Responsibilities

- Hiring
- Revenue growth
- Marketing
- Service expansion
- Operational oversight

#### Pain points

- Inconsistent lead handling
- Poor reporting
- No standardized pipeline
- Marketing activity disconnected from operations
- Office staff using inconsistent processes

#### Effective message

> Give every plumbing inquiry a defined path from first contact to estimate, follow-up, and completed job.

### 7.3 Office manager or dispatcher

#### Responsibilities

- Answering calls
- Scheduling
- Customer communication
- Following up
- Coordinating technicians

#### Pain points

- Conversations across multiple inboxes
- Missing context
- Duplicate work
- No clear status
- After-hours inquiries
- Forgotten estimates

#### Effective message

> Keep customer conversations, appointment requests, and open plumbing opportunities organized in one system.

### 7.4 Marketing-aware decision-maker

#### Responsibilities

- Website
- Reviews
- Advertising
- Lead reporting

#### Pain points

- Cannot connect website activity to customer conversations
- Poor attribution
- Slow website changes
- Weak conversion tracking
- No follow-up after lead generation

#### Effective message

> Connect the website to the CRM and follow-up process so marketing activity becomes visible and actionable.

## 8. Primary Pain Points

### Lead-capture pain

- Website visitors cannot easily request service
- Forms ask too much or too little
- Forms fail on mobile
- Emergency and general requests are not differentiated
- Inquiries arrive without enough context
- No confirmation is sent

### Speed-to-lead pain

- The owner is under a sink, driving, or on another job
- Calls go unanswered
- Leads wait in an inbox
- Prospects call the next plumber
- After-hours inquiries are forgotten

### Sales-process pain

- Estimates are not tracked consistently
- No one knows which opportunities require follow-up
- Lead status depends on memory
- Multiple employees contact the same person
- Lost opportunities are not revisited

### Reputation pain

- Happy customers are not asked for reviews
- Review requests are sent inconsistently
- Negative feedback is discovered publicly
- The company has no service-recovery workflow
- Review activity is not monitored

### Website-management pain

- The current website provider is unresponsive
- The owner cannot make changes
- The site is technically outdated
- The website does not reflect current services
- Branding and business details are inconsistent

### Technology pain

- The company owns disconnected tools
- Employees do not know which system to use
- Software was purchased but never configured
- The owner does not want another platform to learn
- No one maintains automations

## 9. Desired Customer Outcomes

The ideal customer wants:

- A professional website
- More convenient customer contact
- Faster responses
- Fewer lost calls
- Better estimate organization
- More consistent follow-up
- More customer reviews
- Less administrative chaos
- Better visibility into opportunity status
- Less dependence on memory
- A system that works without extensive technical learning
- A provider who manages changes and integrations

The customer may describe these outcomes using simpler language:

- “I want my phone to ring.”
- “I need people to know we received their request.”
- “I’m tired of losing leads.”
- “I need someone to handle the website.”
- “We forget to follow up on estimates.”
- “We need more reviews.”
- “I want everything in one place.”

Sales messaging should use the customer’s operational language.

## 10. Buying Triggers

A buying trigger is an event that increases urgency.

### Website triggers

- Existing website breaks
- Current provider becomes unresponsive
- Domain or hosting issue
- Website looks outdated
- Competitor launches a stronger site
- Mobile form stops working
- Business rebrands
- Services change
- Business expands its service area

### Growth triggers

- Hiring another technician
- Adding vehicles
- Increasing advertising
- Opening a new location
- Adding emergency service
- Expanding into commercial plumbing
- Adding water-heater or sewer services
- Owner stepping away from daily fieldwork

### Operational triggers

- Missed high-value call
- Forgotten estimate
- Customer complaint about communication
- Office staff turnover
- Lead volume becomes difficult to manage
- Existing CRM is unused
- Multiple communication channels become chaotic

### Reputation triggers

- Competitor surpasses the company in reviews
- Negative review goes unanswered
- Review growth becomes stagnant
- Advertising exposes a weak review profile
- Company wants to improve local credibility

## 11. Qualification Questions

### Business viability

- How long has the company been operating?
- Which plumbing services generate most of the work?
- Which areas does the company serve?
- How many technicians or vehicles are active?
- Does the company have capacity for additional work?

### Lead handling

- Who answers new customer calls?
- What happens when a call is missed?
- Where do website inquiries currently go?
- How quickly are new inquiries usually contacted?
- Are after-hours inquiries handled?

### Opportunity management

- How are estimates tracked?
- Who follows up on open estimates?
- How does the company know which leads were won or lost?
- Is there an existing CRM or dispatch platform?

### Website

- Is the company satisfied with the current website?
- Can the company request updates easily?
- Does the website produce inquiries?
- Are forms connected to a follow-up system?
- Does the site accurately reflect current services and locations?

### Reputation

- How are customers asked for reviews?
- Who responds to negative feedback?
- Is the Google review link available?
- Does the company have a legitimate customer database?

### Implementation readiness

- Who will complete onboarding?
- Who can provide domain access?
- Who approves content?
- Who will use the CRM?
- Who should receive notifications?
- Can the company provide licenses and credentials for verification?

## 12. Qualification Scorecard

Score each category from 0–5.

| Category | 0–1 | 2–3 | 4–5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business maturity | New or unverified | Some operating history | Established and verifiable |
| Customer history | No customer base | Limited history | Strong completed-job history |
| Google presence | Missing or problematic | Basic profile | Established profile with legitimate reviews |
| Website need | No clear problem | Moderate weaknesses | Missing, outdated, broken or disconnected |
| Lead-response need | No demand | Some inconsistency | Clear missed-call or response problem |
| Operational capacity | Cannot handle work | Limited capacity | Can respond and fulfill |
| Technology fit | Requires complex systems | Some integration needs | Fits standard system |
| Onboarding readiness | Unresponsive | Some gaps | Decision-maker and access available |
| Budget fit | Resists recurring costs | Uncertain | Understands $297 plus usage |
| Expectation fit | Demands guarantees | Needs education | Accepts managed infrastructure value |

### Score interpretation

| Score | Classification | Action |
|---:|---|---|
| 40–50 | Ideal | Prioritize |
| 30–39 | Qualified | Proceed after resolving gaps |
| 20–29 | Conditional | Require discovery and clear expectations |
| Below 20 | Poor fit | Do not actively pursue |

No score should override legal, ethical, or platform-policy concerns.

## 14. Disqualification Criteria

A prospect should be disqualified or paused when:

- The business cannot be verified
- The company lacks necessary licensing
- The company requests false credentials
- The company requests fake reviews
- The business serves locations it cannot legitimately support
- The company demands guaranteed leads or rankings
- The company expects unlimited customization
- The business refuses required consent and compliance practices
- The company lacks anyone to respond to leads
- The business cannot fulfill additional work
- The owner refuses onboarding
- Required domain or platform access cannot be obtained
- The company requires unsupported enterprise functionality
- The existing system is already stronger and no real problem is identified
- The customer’s expectations cannot fit a $297 productized service

## 15. Negative Customer Personas

### 15.1 The unlaunched plumber

Characteristics:

- No customers
- No reviews
- No service history
- No operating systems
- Expects the website to create the entire business

Risk:

The product improves lead handling but cannot create business viability by itself.

### 15.2 The unlimited-customization buyer

Characteristics:

- Wants a completely bespoke website
- Changes direction repeatedly
- Cannot consolidate feedback
- Expects unlimited revisions

Risk:

Destroys fulfillment economics.

### 15.3 The guaranteed-results buyer

Characteristics:

- Demands guaranteed rankings
- Demands a fixed number of leads
- Holds the provider responsible for sales performance
- Avoids responsibility for answering leads

Risk:

Creates misaligned expectations and high churn.

### 15.4 The nonresponsive owner

Characteristics:

- Does not complete onboarding
- Does not approve content
- Does not answer leads
- Does not update pipeline status
- Blames the system for unused opportunities

Risk:

Prevents implementation and perceived value.

### 15.5 The complex enterprise account

Characteristics:

- Many locations
- Large call center
- Advanced dispatch platform
- Detailed permissions
- Custom reporting
- Procurement and legal requirements

Risk:

May be valuable, but does not fit the base plan.

## 17. Messaging by Pain Point

| Pain point | Message |
|---|---|
| Missed calls | Automatically let missed callers know the company received their call and give them a way to continue the conversation |
| Slow website response | Acknowledge service requests immediately and alert the team |
| Forgotten estimates | Keep open estimates visible and follow up consistently |
| Weak website | Give customers a professional, mobile-friendly way to understand services and request help |
| Low review velocity | Build a consistent process for requesting honest customer reviews |
| Disconnected tools | Keep conversations, contacts, and opportunities in one system |
| Unresponsive web provider | Provide managed website maintenance within defined boundaries |
| Limited technical ability | Install and manage the system instead of giving the owner software to configure |

## 19. Ideal Pilot Customer

The best pilot customer is:

- Owner-led
- Easy to communicate with
- Operating in one primary market
- Established for at least two years
- Has 25 or more legitimate reviews
- Has an outdated or weak website
- Misses calls
- Has no organized lead pipeline
- Can provide assets promptly
- Is willing to test the mobile app
- Is willing to provide honest implementation feedback
- Understands that the product is a pilot
- Does not require custom features

The pilot should favor learning quality over maximum short-term revenue.

## 21. ICP Acceptance Criteria

The ICP is ready for use when:

1. Sales can identify qualified prospects consistently.
2. Public-data scoring can prioritize outreach.
3. Discovery questions expose material fit problems.
4. Disqualification criteria are enforced.
5. Onboarding requirements are explained before purchase.
6. Sales messaging uses operational plumbing language.
7. Pilot customers fall within the defined delivery model.
8. Customer outcomes can be measured without unsupported guarantees.
9. Qualification data is recorded consistently.
10. Churn and support patterns can be compared by segment.
